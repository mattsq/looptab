# M38 — 9×9 Sudoku: test-time compute track (lit-review item 1)

**WHY.** 9×9 Sudoku is the task where the mixer's win survives every control (M33/M34: token-mixing
is the whole win) and there is no trajectory to shortcut it. Recent work suggests two ways to spend
more compute at test time on such a task: per-step noise plus K samples with selection
(Probabilistic TRM, arXiv 2605.19943) and learned memory tokens (arXiv 2604.21999). The ARC study of
TRM (arXiv 2512.11847) warns that the effective recursion can be shallow. Before testing either
idea, M38a re-establishes a properly trained baseline and measures what each loop step does.

## M38a — a properly trained baseline with per-step decoding

Config `configs/experiments/m38a_sudoku9_baseline.yaml`; record
`results/m38a_sudoku9_baseline_20261024T164337*` (RTX 2070, 82 min, mostly puzzle generation).
`n_train` 4000, `n_test` 500, n_givens ∈ {30, 26}, 100 epochs, AMP on every arm, 8 seeds. All four
arms are within ±0.5% of the `trm_flat` budget (323.9k–325.8k parameters).

New substrate (commit `c801f1b`): `extrapolation.param: null` evaluates each arm on the normal
test split after R' loops without changing the task. A test confirms that at the trained depth it
reproduces the main evaluation exactly. `unroll_untied` caps the untied mixer at its 8 blocks.

| n_givens | Δ | accuracy | exact match |
|---|---|---|---|
| 30 | mixer − ff | +0.423 | +0.061 |
| 30 | mixer − flat | +0.455 | +0.061 |

**Reading.**
- **Longer training memorises; it does not solve.** Compared with M34 (25 epochs, 5 seeds, same
  data), the mixer's train accuracy goes 0.938 → 1.000 while test accuracy slightly falls (0.870 →
  0.848 at 30 clues). Exact match rises 0.026 → 0.061, but 94% of test grids still have an error
  (12 wrong cells on average). This is a cross-run comparison with different seed counts. With
  4,000 puzzles, the 9×9 mixer is limited by generalisation, not by fitting.
- **The mixer win is large and controlled** (+0.38 to +0.46 accuracy over `ff` and `trm_flat`). The
  flat loop and `ff` cannot even fit the training set (train acc 0.60–0.68) at this budget.
- **Tying helps on 9×9 Sudoku** (+0.04 to +0.05 accuracy, 8/0 at both difficulties). Both mixers fit
  the training set perfectly, so the gap is in GENERALISATION, not capacity. This differs from
  M24e/M24f, where a budget-matched untied mixer matched the tied loop. It is a second scoped
  exception to §11.2 #7, after M36c.

### Per-step decoding (n_givens = 26 only — see the harness note below)

Test accuracy (exact match) after R' loops, 8 seeds; trained at R = 8:

| R' | 1 | 2 | 4 | 6 | **8** | 12 | 16 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `trm_mixer` | 0.637 (0) | 0.671 (0) | 0.727 (0) | 0.757 (.001) | **0.767 (.009)** | 0.770 (.034) | 0.768 (**.054**) |
| `untied_mixer_matched` | 0.604 | 0.633 | 0.664 | 0.699 | **0.719 (0)** | capped | capped |
| `trm_flat` | 0.356 | 0.355 | 0.356 | 0.360 | **0.362** | 0.361 | 0.357 |
| `ff_matched` (no loop) | 0.392 | — | — | — | — | — | — |

**Reading.**
- **The recursion is NOT shallow on the mixer.** Accuracy climbs monotonically over all eight
  trained steps (0.637 → 0.767, +0.13), the same at every seed (per-seed spread ≤ 0.02). Already at
  step 1 the mixer is far above `ff` (+0.24), so one mixing step does real work, and every later
  step adds more. The flat loop is shallow, exactly as found on ARC: its readout is flat from step 1.
- **Extra test-time loops improve exact match, not cell accuracy.** Beyond the trained depth, cell
  accuracy holds (0.767 → 0.770 → 0.768; 6/2 seeds above R' = 8 at both 12 and 16, not significant),
  but whole-grid exact match rises 0.009 → 0.034 → 0.054, six-fold. The extra loops appear to
  complete grids that were nearly right rather than fixing more cells overall. The record stores
  exact match per seed only as a mean and std, so this rise has no paired sign test yet; at a std of
  0.030 over 8 seeds it is about four standard errors from the R' = 8 value. Treat it as a strong
  lead to confirm, not as a result. This is the first sign in the repo of useful test-time compute
  beyond the trained depth. Unlike on the non-convergent CA (M8/M21/M36), over-unrolling does not
  destroy the answer here.
- The untied mixer improves step by step as well (0.604 → 0.719), but it cannot run past 8 blocks.
  The tied loop's lead over it widens from +0.03 at step 1 to +0.06 at step 4.

## What M38a changed (written before M38b)

- The 9×9 baseline for this track: mixer acc 0.848 / EM 0.061 (30 clues), 0.767 / 0.009 (26 clues),
  train accuracy 1.000. There is plenty of headroom in exact match, and the limit is
  generalisation.
- The mixer's recursion on Sudoku is deep (every trained step helps), and over-unrolling is safe
  and raises exact match. This makes test-time compute a reasonable lever here, unlike on `iterated`.
- Next (M38b): noise per step plus K samples, selected by an exact Sudoku constraint-violation
  count, with three matched-compute controls: K-sample `ff` ensembles, more loops alone (the R' = 16
  curve above), and the untied mixer. A data-scaling point (more puzzles) is also worth one run,
  since the gap is generalisation.

## M38b — training noise, per-step sampling noise, and K samples with selection

Script `experiments/m38_ttc/noise_k_select.py`, analysis `experiments/m38_ttc/analyse.py`; summaries
`results/m38b_noise_k_select_g30_20261024T211129_summary.csv` and
`results/m38b_noise_k_select_g26_20261024T211754_summary.csv` (the per-seed JSON records are
gitignored). Same data, budget, and recipe as M38a (100 epochs, AMP, 4,000 puzzles), seeds 1–8 at
both 30 and 26 clues, about 3.5 hours on the RTX 2070. Run record SHA `b614b88` (clean).

**Design.** Noise is added after each step's normalisation (RMSNorm in the mixers, the hidden GELU in
`ff`): `out += sigma * rms(out) * N(0, 1)`. Each base arm (`trm_mixer`, `untied_mixer_matched`,
`ff_matched`) is trained twice, without noise and with training noise sigma = 0.3, so there are six
arms; noise adds no parameters. At test time each arm is run deterministically (tied mixers for
R' ∈ {8, …, 512}) and with sampling noise (sigma ∈ {0.2, 0.4, 0.8}; R ∈ {8, 16, 32} for the tied
mixers, R = 8 for the others, which cannot run past their depth), drawing K = 32 samples. One answer
is chosen per puzzle by:
- **violation**: fewest Sudoku constraint violations (row, column, and box duplicates, and changed
  givens), ties broken by confidence;
- **confidence**: highest mean log max-probability, with no access to the rules;
- **vote**: per-cell majority over the K samples.

Every test puzzle has a unique solution, so a grid with zero violations that keeps the givens IS the
solution. Violation selection is therefore an exact verifier, and in every cell it equals the oracle
("is any of the K samples correct?", pass@K). Confidence is the verifier-free result.

**Pre-registration.** Seed 0 was a pilot (`results/m38b_pilot_g30_20261024T175917_summary.csv`) used
to choose sigma and R. Before seeds 1–8 ran, the headline cell was fixed at R = 16, sampling sigma =
0.4, K = 32, against the compute-matched control of the deterministic loop at R' = 32 × 16 = 512.
Everything else below is secondary.

### Main evaluation (deterministic, R' = 8)

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 30 | `trm_mixer` | 0.847 ± 0.009 | 1.000 | 0.056 |
| 30 | `trm_mixer` + training noise | **0.878 ± 0.005** | 1.000 | **0.123** |
| 30 | `untied_mixer_matched` | 0.809 | 1.000 | 0.009 |
| 30 | `untied_mixer_matched` + training noise | 0.839 | 1.000 | 0.042 |
| 30 | `ff_matched` | 0.425 | 0.684 | 0 |
| 30 | `ff_matched` + training noise | 0.383 | 0.687 | 0 |
| 26 | `trm_mixer` | 0.767 ± 0.008 | 1.000 | 0.008 |
| 26 | `trm_mixer` + training noise | **0.796 ± 0.002** | 0.998 | 0.013 |
| 26 | `untied_mixer_matched` | 0.717 | 0.998 | 0 |
| 26 | `untied_mixer_matched` + training noise | 0.745 | 1.000 | 0.004 |
| 26 | `ff_matched` | 0.392 | 0.651 | 0 |
| 26 | `ff_matched` + training noise | 0.353 | 0.663 | 0 |

The plain arms reproduce M38a on fresh seeds (mixer 0.847 vs 0.848 at 30 clues). Training noise
raises test accuracy by +0.03 on both mixers (8/0 at both difficulties) while train accuracy stays at
1.0, so it acts as a regulariser on the generalisation gap M38a identified. It hurts `ff` (−0.04,
0/8), which cannot fit the training set even without noise. Tying keeps its edge with or without
noise (+0.04 to +0.05 accuracy, 8/0 in all four comparisons).

### Deterministic over-unrolling (now paired-tested)

Exact match after R' loops (mean of 8 seeds):

| n_givens | arm | 8 | 16 | 32 | 64 | 128 | 512 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 30 | `trm_mixer` | 0.056 | 0.171 | 0.212 | 0.228 | 0.230 | 0.234 |
| 30 | + training noise | 0.123 | 0.448 | 0.513 | 0.521 | 0.521 | 0.522 |
| 26 | `trm_mixer` | 0.008 | 0.053 | 0.081 | 0.089 | 0.092 | 0.092 |
| 26 | + training noise | 0.013 | 0.208 | 0.270 | 0.278 | 0.280 | 0.280 |

R' = 16 and R' = 512 both beat R' = 8 on exact match at 8/0 for both arms and both difficulties, which
confirms the M38a lead. Exact match plateaus by R' ≈ 64, and cell accuracy barely moves (plain mixer
at 30 clues: 0.847 → 0.852 at 16 → 0.846 at 512), so the extra loops finish grids that were nearly
right. The number of violations per grid falls accordingly (plain mixer, 30 clues, seed 1: 6.8 →
3.2 → 2.6 at R' = 8, 16, 32). Training noise more than doubles the plateau (0.234 → 0.522 and
0.092 → 0.280, 8/0): noise during training makes the loop's continuation past its trained depth far
more useful, beyond what it does for the answer at the trained depth.

### Headline: K = 32 samples at R = 16 vs the deterministic loop at R' = 512 (equal compute)

| n_givens | arm | violation | confidence | vote | deterministic R' = 512 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 30 | `trm_mixer` | 0.635 (+0.40) | 0.561 (+0.33) | 0.094 (−0.14) | 0.234 |
| 30 | + training noise | **0.809 (+0.29)** | **0.730 (+0.21)** | 0.395 (−0.13) | 0.522 |
| 26 | `trm_mixer` | 0.293 (+0.20) | 0.249 (+0.16) | 0.013 (−0.08) | 0.092 |
| 26 | + training noise | **0.605 (+0.32)** | **0.531 (+0.25)** | 0.157 (−0.12) | 0.280 |

Exact match; Δ against the deterministic R' = 512 control in brackets. Every Δ is 8/0 or 0/8 (p =
.0078). Violation beats confidence at 8/0 in both noise-trained cells.

**Reading.**
- **Spending compute on K samples beats spending it on more loops, by a wide margin.** At equal
  compute, sampling with selection adds +0.20 to +0.40 exact match over the best the deterministic
  loop reaches, and the deterministic loop has already plateaued by R' = 64, so more loops cannot
  close the gap.
- **The gain does not need the verifier.** Confidence selection, which uses no Sudoku rules, keeps
  70–80% of the verifier's gain (+0.16 to +0.33, all 8/0). The verifier number is pass@32; the
  confidence number is what a model without a rule checker would get.
- **Per-cell voting is harmful** (0/8 in every cell). Independent samples disagree in different
  cells, and a per-cell majority assembles a grid that no sample proposed. Selection must be over
  whole grids.
- **A single sample is worse than the deterministic run** (noise-trained mixer, R = 16: K = 1 gives
  0.381 vs deterministic 0.448 at 30 clues, 0.154 vs 0.208 at 26). The value is in diversity plus
  selection, not in the noise itself. The K curve rises smoothly with no sign of saturation at 32
  (30 clues, violation: 0.381, 0.503, 0.600, 0.679, 0.754, 0.809 for K = 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32).
- **The best combination is training noise plus sampling.** From the plain mixer at its trained
  depth (EM 0.056 at 30 clues, 0.008 at 26), the headline recipe reaches 0.809 and 0.605 with the
  verifier, and 0.730 and 0.531 without it.

### Controls under the same sampling (R = 8, sigma = 0.4, K = 32, violation selection)

| n_givens | `trm_mixer` | + noise | `untied_mixer_matched` | + noise | `ff_matched` | + noise |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 30 | 0.169 | 0.350 | 0.002 | 0.152 | 0 | 0 |
| 26 | 0.017 | 0.067 | 0 | 0.015 | 0 | 0 |

- **`ff` gets nothing from K samples** (EM 0 in every cell): none of its samples is ever fully
  right, so there is nothing for selection to find. The mixing operator is a precondition.
- **Tying matters more for sampling than for accuracy.** At the same depth (8 steps) and the same
  sampling, the tied mixer beats the budget-matched untied mixer by +0.17 / +0.20 exact match at 30
  clues (plain / noise-trained, 8/0) and +0.02 / +0.05 at 26 clues (8/0), against a cell-accuracy
  gap of only +0.04 to +0.05. On top of that, only the tied loop can run past 8 steps, which is
  where most of the gain is (R = 16 vs R = 8 for the noise-trained mixer: 0.809 vs 0.350 at 30
  clues). This is the largest tied-vs-untied gap in the repo outside the step-supervised M36c, and
  extends the scoped exception to §11.2 #7 that M38a found.

### Caveats

- The headline cell was chosen on seed 0 at 30 clues only; the 26-clue results are a fresh
  difficulty with the same settings and agree in every sign.
- Only one training-noise level (0.3) was run on seeds 1–8 (0.1 was tried in the pilot only).
- The deterministic control at R' = 512 is compute-matched in loop steps, not in wall clock; the
  samples run in parallel, the loop does not.
- The verifier result depends on Sudoku having a cheap exact checker. Confidence selection is the
  number that would transfer to tasks without one, and it is still large here.
- 4,000 training puzzles throughout. The remaining errors are still generalisation errors; data
  scaling has not been run.

## What M38b changes

- Test-time compute is a real lever on 9×9 Sudoku, and the way to spend it is diverse samples with
  whole-grid selection, not more deterministic loops. At 30 clues, exact match goes from 0.056
  (plain mixer, trained depth) to 0.73 without a verifier and 0.81 with one, at the compute of a
  512-step loop that reaches 0.234 (0.522 with training noise).
- Training noise is a separate, cheap win: +0.03 cell accuracy on both mixers and a doubled
  over-unrolling plateau.
- Both depend on the mixing operator (`ff` gets nothing) and are much larger for the tied loop than
  for the budget-matched untied mixer.
- Next: M38c (learned memory tokens), a data-scaling point, and a learned selection head (Q-head) to
  compare against confidence selection, since confidence is the verifier-free number.
