## A. Synthetic scale-up: `converge` at larger ring widths (NOT a capacity sweep)

### What was actually varied

M33 already had `converge` at w∈{24,32,48}. M34 adds w∈{64,96}. **`w` is the task's ring width —
the number of output cells `converge` predicts jointly — not a model hyperparameter.** At each
`w`, every arm was RE-WIDENED to stay within ±5% of `trm_flat`'s budget at that `w` (per
`scratchpad/m34/probe_budget_synth.py`), because `trm_flat`'s own parameter count scales with
cell count. So between w=24 and w=96, four things changed at once: number of cells (24→96),
mixer hidden width (per-w budget-matched, not held fixed), reference/flat hidden width (64→96,
tracking `w` directly in these configs), and total parameter budget. Training data was held at a
fixed row count (4000) throughout, so effective samples per output cell also fell as `w` grew.
**This is a task-size sweep with a matched model, not a "does the mechanism need more model
capacity" experiment in the M11 sense** (M11 varied model width at FIXED task). Reported as such:

| w (ring length / cells) | Δ(mixer−nomix) accuracy | Δ(mixer−nomix) EM | mixer accuracy | mixer EM |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 24 (M33) | +0.2921 | +0.9881 | ~0.998 | ~0.990 |
| 32 (M33) | +0.2908 | +0.9759 | ~0.998 | ~0.978 |
| 48 (M33) | +0.2904 | +0.9660 | ~0.998 | ~0.968 |
| 64 (M34) | +0.2892 | +0.9176 | 0.9975 | ~0.920 |
| 96 (M34) | +0.2874 | +0.8089 | 0.9958 | ~0.811 |

All 8/0, p≤.008 at every point (M33/M34 CSVs) — sign-consistent throughout, so this is about
magnitude, not direction. **Defensible claim: per-cell Δ(mixer−nomix) accuracy stayed close to
0.29 as ring length grew from 24 to 96 cells, under these jointly-scaled, budget-matched
models.** It does *not* establish that the mixing mechanism is "capacity-saturated" or has
"reached an asymptote" — no experiment here held the task fixed and varied only model width to
test that. A real capacity test on this axis would fix `w` (and training data) and sweep only
`hidden_dim`/`latent_dim` at that fixed task, the way M11 did on a different task.

Two more honest observations that don't need the capacity framing:

- **Whole-row EM declines as ring length grows** (0.988→0.809, monotonic across all 5 points),
  while per-cell accuracy is roughly flat. This is arithmetic, not a new mechanism: `trm_mixer`'s
  own per-cell accuracy drifts slightly (0.998→0.996) as `w` grows, and raising a ~99.8%→99.6%
  per-cell rate to a higher power (more cells that all must be simultaneously correct)
  mechanically amplifies a small per-cell regression into a large whole-row-EM one. Whether that
  small per-cell drift itself reflects undertraining at fixed epoch count (100 epochs at every
  `w`) or something else about the larger/harder task instance is not distinguished by this
  experiment either.
- `trm_mixer_nomix_distinctw` at w=96 breaches budget parity (ratio 0.8975, >5% under) — the
  same coarse per-cell-weight-quantization artifact M33 documented on binary cells; moot here too
  since Δ(nomix−distinctw) = 0.0000 at every width (degenerate saturation, identical to M33's
  finding — the three CI-body arms converge to the same per-cell decision regardless of
  parameterization).

### `sudoku9`: first classic 9x9 Sudoku run in this repo — mixing replicates, but `nomix` is a trivial baseline in disguise

5 seeds (exploratory, per the config's own scope — n_train=4000/epochs=25 chosen for tractable
generation time on 9x9, not a significance target), n_givens∈{30,26,24}. **n_givens is a clue
count, not a validated difficulty label** — read the three points as three train/test instances,
not a calibrated "medium→hard→very-hard" ladder (no independent hardness measure was computed).

| n_givens | mixer acc | mixer blank-only acc (est.) | mixer EM (exact solve) | nomix acc | nomix vs. trivial-baseline acc |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 30 | 0.870 | ~79% | 2.56% | 0.4401 | 0.4403 (copy givens + guess blanks uniformly) |
| 26 | 0.797 | ~70% | ~0% | 0.3957 | 0.3964 |
| 24 | 0.771 | ~68% | ~0% | 0.3744 | 0.3745 |

(Blank-only accuracy is a back-calculation from whole-grid accuracy assuming near-perfect given
reproduction — `mean_wrong_per_row` at n_givens=30 is 10.53/81, consistent with the 0.870 figure
— not a directly reported metric; treat as an estimate.)

**`trm_mixer_nomix`'s whole-grid accuracy is statistically indistinguishable from a model that
has learned nothing beyond "reproduce the input, guess randomly among 9 digits everywhere
else."** This is the channel-independent control doing exactly what §11.2 #8's dividing line
predicts (Sudoku's constraints are the definition of cross-cell coupling; a channel-independent
model structurally cannot see them) — but it means the `Δ(nomix−ff)` "CI beats a plain MLP"
comparison on this task is a comparison between two arms that are BOTH close to a trivial
baseline, not a meaningful architecture contest; treat that leg's small positive delta
(+0.04–0.06, matching M33's direction) as weak, not a real finding on its own.

**`trm_mixer` is doing real, non-trivial constraint-informed prediction** (blank-only accuracy
~68–79%, well above the 1/9 ≈ 11% chance rate that `nomix` sits at), and mixing dominates the
accuracy-level comparison exactly as M33 found on every other task (+0.40–0.43, matching M33's
established magnitude). **But it is very rarely solving the whole grid** — exact-match (genuine
full-grid solves) is 2.56% at the easiest point tested and effectively 0% at the two harder
points. This is a real, useful result (mixing clearly helps prediction quality at 9x9 scale) but
it is NOT a "9x9 Sudoku solved" result, and the write-up should not conflate per-cell accuracy
with solving. A genuine solve-rate win at this scale would need more training data/epochs than
this exploratory pass budgeted. Readout/weight-share legs again degenerate-saturate to Δ≈0,
matching M33.

## B. Forecasting breadth: does the M31/M32 pattern generalize?

### `etth2`/`ettm1`/`ettm2` (M=7, same geometry as `etth1`): the CI leg replicates cleanly; the mixing-harm leg does NOT (post-review correction)

Full 10 seeds each, `n_folds=10`, horizon=24 (never run before at this horizon even for
`etth1`), same training recipe (25 epochs, full fp32/`cuda_graph`) as the established M=7–21
configs. **Only one of the two M31/M32 legs replicates across all three** — see below,
corrected after review flagged the original "both legs replicate" framing as too strong:

| dataset | Δ(mixer−nomix) MSE [mixing] | sign | Δ(nomix−ff) MSE [CI] | sign |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| etth2 | +0.0084 (harmful) | 9/1, p=.021/.039 robust | −0.1252 (CI wins) | 10/0, p=.002 |
| ettm1 | +0.0364 (harmful, ns) | 6/4, p=.75 | −0.1219 (CI wins) | 10/0, p=.002 |
| ettm2 | +0.0055 (harmful, ns) | 5/5, p=1.0 | −0.1532 (CI wins) | 10/0, p=.002 |

**The channel-independence-beats-a-plain-MLP leg replicates cleanly** — 10/0 sign-consistent
on all three new datasets, matching M31/M32's `etth1`/`weather` result — but read the p-values as
*indicative*, per the repo's own convention (`real.py`'s docstring: nested/overlapping train sets
across folds, cf. Dietterich 1998), not as an exact significance test, and note each fold trains
exactly ONE model init, so temporal-block variance and initialization variance are not separated
here. **The mixing-is-harmful leg does NOT cleanly replicate — it appears on `etth2` (9/1,
nominally significant) and is UNRESOLVED, not weakly-present, on `ettm1`/`ettm2`.** A 5/5 sign
split (`ettm2`) is a null result, not weak directional evidence — calling it "directionally
consistent" because the mean happens to be positive overstates what a 50/50 sign count actually
shows. The honest read: mixing's harm at M=7 is real on at least one dataset (`etth2`, matching
M31/M32's own `etth1` read, 8/2) but is not established as a general M=7 property across this
dataset family — it may be etth-family-specific (etth1/etth2 share an hourly-sampling, oil-temp
schema that ettm1/ettm2's 15-minute sampling doesn't) rather than a clean function of channel
count alone.

### `electricity` (M=321) / `traffic` (M=862): mixing looks helpful here — but three things changed at once, not just channel count

The reason this milestone exists — extending M32's two-point channel-count observation (weather
M=21 vs etth1 M=7). **EXPLORATORY, 4 seeds — read directionally, not as a significance claim, and
read the scope narrowly — see the confounds below before treating this as a "channel count"
result:**

| dataset (M) | Δ(mixer−nomix) MSE [mixing] | sign | Δ(nomix−ff) MSE [CI] | sign |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| electricity (321) | −0.0271 (mixing helps, these 4 blocks) | 4/0 | −0.0926 (CI wins) | 4/0 |
| traffic (862) | −0.0450 (mixing helps, these 4 blocks) | 4/0 | −0.4335 (CI wins, but see FF caveat) | 4/0 |

**What changed between this tier and the M=7–21 tier is NOT only channel count:**

- **Training recipe**: 15 epochs + AMP + gradient-accumulated microbatching here, vs 25 epochs +
  full fp32 + `cuda_graph` at M=7–21 — a real precision/optimization-budget difference, not an
  incidental detail.
- **Which test data**: `fold = seed % n_folds` (`real.py`), and seeds `[0,1,2,3]` select the
  FIRST four of ten chronological backtest blocks — i.e., specifically the EARLIEST test period,
  not a random or representative sample across the full ten-block span the M=7–21 configs (10
  seeds, all ten blocks) covered.
- **Dataset identity and correlation structure** differ from the ETT family regardless of channel
  count (electricity/traffic are grid/network sensor data; ETT is 6 loads + oil temperature from
  transformers) — "more channels" and "a different kind of real-world process" are not separable
  from these two datasets alone.

**The properly scoped claim is: on the four earliest chronological blocks of `electricity` and
`traffic`, under the AMP/15-epoch/microbatched training recipe, mixing helped (4/4 seeds on
both) — the opposite direction from every M=7–21 configuration tested (which used a different
recipe and all ten blocks).** Whether that reversal is caused by channel count, by the training
recipe, by which time period was sampled, by the specific datasets, or some combination is NOT
established here — the experiment as designed cannot separate these. A plausible hypothesis
(hundreds of correlated real-world channels on a shared grid/network may carry more genuinely
exploitable cross-variable structure than the ETT family's 7 loosely related variables) remains a
hypothesis, not a finding. **A causal channel-count test would need**: channel-subsampled draws
from the SAME high-M dataset at several sizes, the SAME training recipe and epoch count across
every size, all ten chronological blocks (or an equal, randomly-drawn subset at every size), and
ideally multiple inits per fold to separate init variance from block variance.

**The CI-beats-FF leg needs a further caveat on `traffic` specifically**: `ff_matched`'s own
TRAIN mse (0.685) is worse than `trm_mixer_nomix`'s TEST mse (0.377) — `ff_matched` does not
appear to have converged within the shared 15-epoch budget on this dataset (electricity is
milder: ff train mse 0.166 vs nomix train mse 0.137, a smaller and more plausibly real gap). The
traffic CI-vs-FF delta is therefore at least partly "CI optimizes faster/easier under this
recipe," not cleanly an architecture-only generalization advantage. This caveat does **not**
touch the mixer-vs-nomix mixing-sign comparison above, which never involves `ff_matched`. See the
follow-up experiments below for a direct test of both caveats.

## Follow-up experiments (post-review, cheap probes)

Two of the review's caveats are directly testable cheaply. Both run.

### Recipe-isolation probe: what does it actually show? (narrowed after review)

`m34_etth2_h24_amprecipe.yaml` — identical to `m34_etth2_h24.yaml` (same dataset, widths, 10
seeds, all deltas) with the training recipe changed to match electricity/traffic's epoch count
and precision (epochs 15 not 25, `amp: true` not `cuda_graph: true`). **Caveat that narrows this
probe's scope, flagged on review: it does NOT match electricity/traffic's recipe completely** —
`etth2`'s widths (max hidden_dim 224) never need `microbatch_size`, which the real
electricity/traffic configs use on every wide arm. So this tests "epochs + precision," not the
full recipe bundle including gradient-accumulated microbatching.

| recipe | Δ(mixer−nomix) MSE | sign | Δ(nomix−ff) MSE | sign |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| original (25ep, cuda_graph) | +0.0084 (harmful) | 9/1, p=.021/.039 robust | −0.1252 (CI wins) | 10/0, p=.002 |
| amp recipe (15ep, amp) | +0.0038 (harmful, weaker) | 6/4, p=.75/.73 robust | −0.1109 (CI wins) | 10/0, p=.002 |

**Result: the recipe change weakens the mixing-harmful signal (a nominally-significant 9/1
collapses to a noise-level 6/4 coin flip) but does NOT flip its sign to helpful.** The CI-beats-ff
leg is essentially unaffected by the recipe change (−0.125 → −0.111, still 10/0).

**What this does and does NOT establish (corrected — the original write-up overstated this):**
it shows that on `etth2` specifically, switching epochs+precision alone does not reverse the
mixing sign. **It does NOT rule out recipe as an explanation for the electricity/traffic
reversal in general.** Untested and left open: recipe×dataset interactions, recipe×channel-count
interactions (the same recipe change could behave differently at M=321/862 than at M=7), whether
convergence rates themselves differ at high M in a way that matters here, and — since this probe
never engages microbatching — whether gradient-accumulated training itself (not just
epochs/precision) contributes to the electricity/traffic reversal. The properly scoped claim is
narrow: "on `etth2`, epochs+precision alone isn't sufficient to flip the sign" — not "recipe is
ruled out as a driver of the reversal."

### FF-convergence probe: is `ff_matched` just undertrained at 15 epochs?

`m34_electricity_ffconverge.yaml` / `m34_traffic_ffconverge.yaml` — 3 arms only (`ff_matched`,
`trm_mixer_nomix`, `trm_flat` as budget reference), same widths/recipe as the M34 forecasting
configs, epochs tripled (15→45), 3 seeds (diagnostic, not claim-grade).

| dataset | arm | train mse @15ep (orig, mean) | train mse @45ep (mean) | train mse @45ep (per-seed range) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| electricity | ff_matched | 0.166 | 0.123 | 0.115–0.127 (stable) |
| electricity | trm_mixer_nomix | 0.137 | 0.110 | 0.104–0.114 (stable) |
| traffic | ff_matched | 0.685 | 0.496 | **0.314–0.772 (2.5x range)** |
| traffic | trm_mixer_nomix | 0.278 | 0.224 | 0.219–0.228 (stable) |

**Electricity: a clean, partial "undertrained" story.** `ff_matched`'s train mse improves 26%
with 3x epochs, converges reliably (tight across seeds, like `nomix`/`trm_flat`), and the CI gap
narrows but does not close (Δ(nomix−ff) MSE −0.0926 → −0.0797, same direction, ~14% smaller,
`ff_matched` train mse 0.123 still meaningfully above `nomix`'s 0.110). More epochs help, but
CI genuinely fits better even once you control for that.

**Traffic: `ff_matched` improves with more training on EVERY seed, but stays far more sensitive to
which seed than `nomix`/`trm_flat` — and the paired data does NOT support calling this
"optimization instability" specifically (corrected after review).** Paired per-seed comparison
(this is the number that matters, not a comparison to a cross-seed mean):

| seed | ff train mse @15ep | ff train mse @45ep | ff test mse @15ep | ff test mse @45ep |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0.400 | 0.314 | 0.538 | 0.480 |
| 1 | 0.811 | 0.772 | 0.899 | 0.863 |
| 2 | 0.527 | 0.401 | 0.734 | 0.613 |

**Every seed improves from 15→45 epochs** — the original write-up compared seed 1's 45-epoch
value (0.772) to the 15-epoch CROSS-SEED MEAN (0.685) and concluded "more training did not
reliably help," which is an apples-to-oranges comparison; paired correctly, seed 1 also improved
(0.811→0.772), just modestly, and remains persistently the hardest seed at both budgets. The
defensible statement is: **FF improves with more epochs on every paired seed, but remains
substantially worse than `nomix`/`trm_flat` and unusually sensitive to which seed, across the
combined fold+initialization runs.**

**Why not "optimization instability":** in this codebase, `seed` simultaneously sets the model's
`torch.manual_seed` AND (via `fold = seed % n_folds`) which chronological test/train block is
used (`run.py`/`real.py`) — the two are perfectly confounded here. So the 0.314–0.772 spread
could reflect init sensitivity, could reflect that fold 1's time period is intrinsically harder
to fit, or could reflect an architecture×time-period interaction specific to `ff_matched`'s
parameterization — this experiment cannot distinguish those. What IS informative: `nomix`/
`trm_flat`, trained on the SAME three folds under the SAME recipe, show no comparable spread
(train mse ranges of 0.009 and 0.001 respectively) — so whatever is driving `ff_matched`'s
sensitivity is specific to `ff_matched`, not a property of fold 1 that affects every architecture.
**Isolating the actual mechanism (init vs. fold vs. interaction) needs multiple initialization
seeds evaluated on ONE fixed fold — not run here, and not currently supported by the config
schema (`seed` controls both); a real fix would need a way to vary model init independently of
the data fold.** The point-estimate CI-vs-FF gap narrows at 45 epochs (Δ(nomix−ff) MSE
−0.4335 → −0.2988, ~31% smaller) but carries high variance in both directions (±0.1952 vs
±0.2586) driven by `ff_matched`'s own spread. **Conclusion (superseded by the fixed-fold probe directly below — kept for the record): the
traffic CI-beats-FF delta is real in direction and improves with training, but `ff_matched`'s
numbers are noisy and their exact source (init vs. fold vs. interaction) is unresolved — prefer
`nomix`/`trm_flat` (both stable across seeds) as the more trustworthy reference points on this
dataset.**

## Third round: resolving the open mechanisms (three more cheap probes)

The second review pass left three things explicitly open: whether traffic's `ff_matched`
variance is init-driven, whether microbatching itself is a hidden recipe factor, and whether the
recipe's attenuating effect on mixing-harm scales with channel count. All three were cheap to
test directly.

### Multi-init at a FIXED fold: traffic `ff_matched`'s variance IS init-driven (v2, properly controlled)

**★ v1 of this probe (results below, kept for the record) had a real methodological flaw, caught
on a third review pass: `torch.manual_seed(init_seed)` was set once before training, but
`InMemoryLoader.__iter__` (`data/dataset.py`) draws its per-epoch shuffle permutation from the
GLOBAL torch RNG on every epoch — so varying `init_seed` varied both the model's initial weights
AND the entire minibatch order across all 45 epochs. The v1 conclusion ("init-driven") was
therefore not fully earned: it had shown *training-stochasticity* sensitivity, not specifically
*initialization* sensitivity. v1 also only tested `ff_matched`, so "specific to ff_matched"
rested on an unmatched comparison (this arm's fixed-fold/multi-init spread vs. other arms'
different-fold/single-init spread), and it only printed to stdout with no run-record artifact
(§5.7 violation).**

**v2 fixes all three**: resets the global RNG to a FIXED, init-independent value
(`torch.manual_seed(999)`) immediately after model construction and before `train()`, so every
run replays an IDENTICAL shuffle sequence and only the starting weights differ; adds `trm`
(labelled `trm_flat` in configs) as a control arm under the IDENTICAL fixed-fold/multi-init
protocol; writes a JSON run record (git_sha, fold, config, per-init metrics) to `results/`
(`m34_traffic_ff_multiinit_20260816T192827.json`). Same `fold=1`, same 5 init seeds:

mechanism claim.**

### Microbatching-in-isolation on `etth2`: no meaningful effect

### Weather recipe-isolation pair: a fourth data point — and a THIRD-ROUND correction to how it was read

`m34_weather_h24.yaml` (baseline: 25ep, `cuda_graph`) / `m34_weather_h24_amprecipe.yaml` (matched
recipe: 15ep, `amp`) — a third, previously-untested channel count (M=21) between `etth2` (M=7)
and `electricity` (M=321), at H=24 (never run before at this horizon for `weather` either).

| dataset (M) | recipe | Δ(mixer−nomix) MSE | sign | Δ(nomix−ff) MSE | sign |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| etth2 (7) | baseline (25ep, cuda_graph) | +0.0084 | 9/1, p=.021 | −0.1252 | 10/0, p=.002 |
| etth2 (7) | amp (15ep, amp) | +0.0038 | 6/4, p=.75 | −0.1109 | 10/0, p=.002 |
| weather (21) | baseline (25ep, cuda_graph) | +0.0112 | 7/3, p=.34 | −0.2188 | 9/1, p=.021 |
| weather (21) | amp (15ep, amp) | −0.0029 | 5/5, p=1.0 | −0.1665 | 10/0, p=.002 |
| electricity (321) | amp+microbatch (15ep) | −0.0271 | 4/0, p=.125 | −0.0926 | 4/0, p=.125 |
| traffic (862) | amp+microbatch (15ep) | −0.0450 | 4/0, p=.125 | −0.4335 | 4/0, p=.125 |

**★ CORRECTED (third review pass) — this table was previously read as a "4-point monotonic
dose-response curve" and treated as meaningfully stronger causal evidence for a channel-count
effect. That was overstated on two counts, both confirmed on inspection:**

1. **This remains an ECOLOGICAL comparison, not a channel-count experiment.** Each M value is a
   DIFFERENT dataset (`etth2`, `weather`, `electricity`, `traffic`) — channel count is still
   perfectly confounded with dataset identity, domain, correlation structure, and sampling
   frequency, exactly as it was before the weather probe. Holding the training recipe constant
   removed ONE confound (recipe), not all of them. Calling the pattern a "dose response" or
   channel count "the best available explanation" implied a causal claim four different datasets
   cannot support. The correctly scoped statement is: *"under a held AMP recipe, these four
   dataset-level means happen to be numerically monotonic with channel count."* That is a
   descriptive observation, not evidence of a mechanism.
2. **The two low-M points are themselves NULL results.** `etth2` amp: +0.0038, sign 6/4 (p=.75).
   `weather` amp: −0.0029, sign 5/5 (p=1.0, a dead-even split). Both are indistinguishable from
   zero individually. The apparent "sign crossing" between them (+0.0038 → −0.0029) is a
   ±0.003–0.004 wobble between two noise-level estimates, not attenuation — reading it as a trend
   was an overclaim. Only `electricity`/`traffic` show a real (if still exploratory, 4-seed)
   signal in the helpful direction.

**What survives:** the two high-M points are real and sign-consistent (4/0 both), and they are
numerically further from zero and same-signed with the two (null) low-M points' point estimates.
That is weak, suggestive, non-causal evidence — nothing stronger. **The channel-subsampling
experiment (draw multiple random channel subsets from the SAME high-M dataset at several sizes,
one held recipe, all ten blocks) is still required to make a causal channel-count claim, and is
NOT superseded by this table.**

**The proposed recipe×channel-count INTERACTION is also not supported once uncertainty is
computed properly (also corrected — the original claim was hypothesis dressed as a finding).**
A difference-in-differences using the reported per-config means/stds (10 seeds each, standard
error = std/√10, propagated in quadrature across the 4 independent estimates):

```
DiD = (amp_weather − amp_etth2) − (base_weather − base_etth2)
    = (−0.0029 − 0.0038) − (0.0112 − 0.0084)
    = −0.0095 ± 0.0115 (SE)      95% CI ≈ [−0.032, +0.013]      t ≈ −0.83
```

The estimated interaction is small relative to its own uncertainty and the CI comfortably
crosses zero. **There is no statistical support for a recipe×channel-count interaction in this
data** — the apparent asymmetry (AMP shows attenuation M=7→21, baseline doesn't) is well within
what two noisy null-ish point estimates per recipe would produce by chance. Retracted: "AMP may
be a necessary condition for the effect to appear."

The CI-beats-ff leg is unaffected by any of the above — sign-consistent and significant
(indicative p) at every point except the exploratory 4-seed high-M cells, regardless of recipe
or channel count.

## Fourth round: channel-subsampling within ONE dataset — real progress, but NOT a clean channel-count isolation (★ CORRECTED, fourth review pass)

Every "channel count" comparison above — including the retracted "dose response" — compared
DIFFERENT datasets. This round drew random subsets of `M` channels from the SAME dataset,
intended to hold dataset identity, domain, correlation structure, and sampling frequency fixed so
only channel count varies. **A fourth external review caught that this intent was not achieved,
and that the headline "generalizes to traffic" claim used the wrong statistic. Both fixed below.**

`scratchpad/m34/channel_subsample_probe.py` draws a random subset of `M` channels from a real
dataset's full channel set, reconstructs the same expanding-window backtest/standardization logic
`make_forecast_splits` uses, and runs the core two-arm comparison (`trm_mixer` vs
`trm_mixer_nomix`) under the SAME AMP/15-epoch recipe as the full-dataset runs. 5 seeds
(exploratory). Extended (same session) to a second independent channel draw at electricity M=64
and the same M=7/M=64 subsampling on `traffic`.

**★ What "only channel count varies" got wrong (verified, not just conceded):**
- **Channel composition is NOT nested between M=7 and M=64.** Each draw calls
  `rng.choice(M_full, size=M_sub, replace=False)` independently per `M`, so the M=7 set is not a
  subset of the M=64 set. Checked directly: of electricity's 7 selected M=7 channels
  (`[27,28,138,139,207,244,274]`), only **3** (`139,207,244`) also appear in the 64-channel draw.
  Growing `M` therefore changes WHICH channels are included, not just how many — a real,
  distinct confound from channel count itself.
- **Model capacity scales sharply with M, by design (budget-matching), but this means capacity is
  NOT held fixed across the M points being compared.** Recorded widths: M=7 → hidden=224/latent=96
  (~73k params) → M=64 → hidden=latent=500 (~606k) → M=321 (electricity full) → hidden=latent=1110
  (~3.0M) → M=862 (traffic full) → hidden=latent=1760 (~8.0M). Budget-matching mixer-vs-nomix
  *within* each M point is correct practice (§4 control contract), but it means the *between-M*
  comparison this section is built on confounds channel count with a ~100x swing in model width —
  exactly the same class of error as the retracted `converge` "capacity" claim (Finding #2, third
  review pass), reintroduced in a new experiment.
- **The full-dataset endpoints (M=321, M=862) use `microbatch_size` gradient accumulation; the
  subsampled points (M=7, M=64) do not** (not needed for memory at those widths). The
  microbatch-isolation probe found no effect at M=7 (§ above), but that was never tested at the
  intermediate/full widths, so it is not established that microbatching is inert everywhere on
  this axis either.

**Correct characterization: this is evidence for an M-associated trend under JOINTLY changing
channel composition, model capacity, and (at the full endpoint) optimization mechanics — not a
causal isolation of channel count.** The earlier framing ("only channel count varies", "the actual
causal test") is retracted.

**★ The traffic "generalizes" claim tested the wrong quantity — recomputed as paired per-seed
contrasts (the statistic the channel-count hypothesis actually concerns: does the mixing benefit
GROW between M values, seed-for-seed), not just each M's own mean/sign:**

| dataset | contrast | Δ(Δ mixer−nomix) mean | sign | exact 2-sided p |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| electricity | M=7→M=64 (draw seed 42) | −0.0128 | 5/0 | .0625 |
| electricity | M=7→M=64 (draw seed 123, independent M=64 redraw) | −0.0092 | 5/0 | .0625 |
| traffic | M=7→M=64 | −0.0075 | 4/1 | .375 |
| traffic | M=64→full (seeds 0–3 overlap only) | −0.0155 | **2/2** | **1.0** |

**Electricity's paired contrast is directionally consistent (5/0 twice) though not significant at
n=5, and both draws reuse the SAME M=7 baseline (not an independent re-draw at M=7, a remaining
gap) — promising exploratory evidence, not proof.** **Traffic's paired contrasts do NOT support a
growing effect**: M=7→M=64 is a weak 4/1 (p=.375, not distinguishable from noise), and critically
M=64→full is an EXACT 2/2 split (p=1.0) — a dead null when tested at the seed level, even though
the raw point-estimate means happened to be ordered in the "expected" direction. **"Generalizes to
a second dataset" is downgraded to: traffic's per-M means are numerically ordered the same way as
electricity's, but the paired seed-level evidence does not support a statistically consistent
growing channel-count effect on traffic.**

**What survives, stated at the confidence level it earns:**
- A genuine, first-of-its-kind within-dataset (not cross-dataset) signal on `electricity`,
  directionally consistent across two independent M=64 channel draws (paired contrast negative
  5/0 both times) — the best evidence for a channel-count-associated trend in this file, still
  confounded with channel composition and model capacity as detailed above, and not yet
  significant (p=.0625, n=5).
- No comparable within-dataset evidence on `traffic` once tested correctly — the traffic numbers
  are consistent with noise once paired.
- The overall channel-count hypothesis is therefore still open, now on more precise footing:
  electricity offers suggestive (not proven, not isolated) support; traffic offers none once
  measured correctly.

**Honest caveats (expanded after the fourth review pass):**
- Channel composition, model capacity, and (at the endpoints) microbatching all co-vary with M —
  a genuinely clean test would fix model width and use NESTED channel subsets (e.g., M=64's set
  contains M=7's set) across M, which this design did not do.
- Each subsample point is 5 seeds, exploratory; both electricity M=64 draws were tested against
  the SAME single M=7 draw, not an independently redrawn one.
- Only two M values tested per dataset (plus the full-M anchor); no denser sweep.
- This tests the MIXING-SIGN leg only; it does not repeat the full 7-arm decomposition at any
  subsampled size.
- A genuinely isolating design (fixed model width across M, nested channel subsets, matched
  training mechanics throughout) remains undone.

## Net read (twice-revised — see the two correction passes above)

- **Direction A did not test what it set out to test.** The `converge` width sweep varies task
  size with a matched model, not model capacity at fixed task — it cannot answer "does the mixing
  edge grow with capacity," and the original write-up's "capacity-invariant/saturated" language
  is retracted. What it DOES show: per-cell Δ(mixer−nomix) stays close to 0.29 as ring length
  grows from 24 to 96 cells under jointly-scaled, budget-matched models — a real, useful
  extension of M33's range, just not a capacity finding.
- **Direction B's CI-beats-plain-MLP leg is robust on the three ETT-family datasets specifically**
  (`etth2`/`ettm1`/`ettm2`, full 10 seeds, 10/0 sign-consistent, matching `etth1`/`weather`). Its
  extension to `electricity`/`traffic` (4/0 both) is real and sign-consistent but stays
  EXPLORATORY: only 4 folds (not 10), and `traffic`'s `ff_matched` reference is confirmed noisy
  (see below) — so "robust across five datasets" overstates the high-M evidence; call it "robust
  on 3, exploratory-but-consistent on 2."
- **The mixing-is-harmful leg does NOT cleanly replicate even within the low-channel tier** —
  present on `etth2` (9/1), a genuine null on `ettm1`/`ettm2` (6/4, 5/5 — a coin flip is a null,
  not weak evidence in a direction). Its status may be etth-family-specific rather than a clean
  function of channel count.
- **★ CORRECTED (third review pass): the "mixing reverses sign at high channel count" claim is
  NOT backed by a dose-response curve — that framing has been retracted.** Each channel-count
  point is still a different dataset (`etth2`/`weather`/`electricity`/`traffic`); holding the
  recipe constant removed only ONE of several confounds (dataset identity, correlation structure,
  sampling frequency remain entangled with M). The two low-M points (`etth2` amp 6/4, `weather`
  amp 5/5) are individually null — a sign wobble between two noise-level estimates is not
  evidence of a trend. What remains real: `electricity`/`traffic` show a consistent (4/0 both),
  though still exploratory, helpful-direction signal, numerically in the same direction as (but
  not causally linked to) the two null low-M points. **The channel-subsampling experiment (below)
  is a first pass at the design this pointed to — it partially, not fully, addresses this gap.**
- **★ TWICE-CORRECTED: the channel-subsampling probes are real progress but do NOT isolate
  channel count, and the traffic "generalization" claim used the wrong statistic (fourth review
  pass).** Channel composition is not nested across M (only 3/7 electricity M=7 channels also
  appear in the M=64 draw), model width scales ~100x with M by budget-matching design (224/96 →
  500/500 → 1110/1110 → 1760/1760), and the full endpoints use microbatching while the subsamples
  don't — so this is evidence for an M-associated trend under jointly-varying composition,
  capacity, and mechanics, not a clean causal isolation. Properly paired per-seed contrasts (the
  statistic the hypothesis actually concerns) show: **electricity M=7→M=64 is directionally
  consistent** (−0.0128 and −0.0092 on two independent M=64 draws, 5/0 both times, p=.0625 each,

  M=64→full is an exact 2/2 (p=1.0) — a dead null once paired at the seed level, even though the

  "traffic's per-M means are numerically ordered the same way; the paired evidence doesn't support
  a real effect there." See "Fourth round" for the full analysis and remaining caveats.
- **★ CORRECTED: the proposed recipe×channel-count interaction has no statistical support.** A
  difference-in-differences with propagated uncertainty (DiD = −0.0095 ± 0.0115 SE, 95% CI
  ≈[−0.032, +0.013], t≈−0.83) shows the apparent asymmetry between recipes is well within noise.
  Retracted: "AMP may be a necessary condition for the effect to appear" — this was hypothesis
  framed as a finding.
- **Microbatching-in-isolation on `etth2`** (forced onto arms that don't need it for memory)
  found no meaningful shift — both with and without land in the same noise-level-null regime —
  ruling out gradient accumulation itself as a hidden driver, at least at this channel count.
- **Traffic `ff_matched`'s instability is init-driven and arm-specific on the (post-hoc-selected)
