# M36 — depth extrapolation re-tested on the cross-cell MIXER (Task B, `iterated`): the first transferable step operator in the repo — but ONLY with step-aligned supervision

**WHY.** Every depth-extrapolation null (M1/M3a/M3b/M7/M8, §11.2 #4) was measured on the flat `trm`,
which §4 now calls feedforward-equivalent. M24 put `trm_mixer` on `iterated` but only in-distribution
(loop depth coupled to T, no over-unroll). A 2026 preprint, "When Does Recurrence Become an
Algorithm? Convergence Selection in Weight-Tied Looped Transformers" (arXiv 2607.20594), reports
that weight tying selects a serial step-wise solution, that extra test-time loops recover longer
computations, that a depth-matched untied model extrapolates worst, and that an "operator-first"
curriculum removes a learning barrier. M36 asks the §3 extrapolation question of the mixer.

**Setup (both runs).** Rule 110, w=24, distractors=0, n_train=4000, n_test=1000, 100 epochs, 8 seeds,
CPU, `parallel_workers: 4`. Every matched arm within ±1.2% of the `trm_flat` reference budget
(16,176 params). Extrapolation test rows come from the held-out `test_sample_seed`, same task
function. Classification sign: positive Δ favours the first arm. Sign test is the two-sided exact
binomial (8/0 ⇒ p=.0078).

| majority baseline | 0.535 | | | 0.537 |

**Reading.**
- In-distribution the M24 result reproduces: Δ(trm_mixer − ff) = +0.238 acc, 8/0; Δ(trm_mixer − trm_flat) = +0.242, 8/0.
- **No extrapolation at all, for any arm.** At T=6 (two CA steps past training) every arm at every
  R' sits at the baseline band (0.50–0.556); EM = 0 everywhere at T ≥ 6.
- **Over-unrolling destroys the in-distribution answer.** `trm_mixer` at T=4 falls 0.903 → 0.539 at
  R'=6. The `trm_mixer_r8` arm peaks exactly at its trained R'=8 (0.735 → 0.840 → 0.936 → 0.708 at

- **Tying HURTS in distribution here:** the budget-matched untied mixer beats the tied one,
  Δ(trm_mixer − untied_mixer_matched) = −0.082 acc / −0.636 EM, 0/8. (2607.20594's tied-wins claim
  concerns extrapolation, where both are at baseline, so this does not test it.)

## M36b — T-curriculum (T ~ U{1..8}) + STEP-ALIGNED deep supervision (loop step i ↔ s_i)

The M3b recipe carried to the mixer — the closest in-repo analogue of an operator-first curriculum.
Config `configs/experiments/m36b_mixer_stepds_curriculum.yaml`; record
`results/m36b_mixer_stepds_curriculum_20261024T023808*` (wall clock 13.3 min). T_test ∈ {12, 16} is OOD.

Diagonal R' = T (one loop per CA step):

| arm | T=4 acc / EM | T=8 acc / EM | **T=12 acc / EM (OOD)** | **T=16 acc / EM (OOD)** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `trm_mixer_stepds` | 0.998 / 0.965 | 0.992 / 0.939 | **0.983 / 0.886** | **0.966 / 0.823** |
| `trm_mixer_finalds` | 0.818 / 0.043 | 0.666 / 0.002 | 0.557 / 0 | 0.529 / 0 |
| `trm_flat_stepds` | 0.846 / 0.053 | 0.595 / 0 | 0.514 / 0 | 0.518 / 0 |
| `untied_mixer_matched` (depth 8, DS off) | 0.586 / 0 | 0.580 / 0 | 0.544 / 0 | 0.536 / 0 |
| `ff_matched` | 0.578 / 0 | 0.557 / 0 | 0.536 / 0 | 0.538 / 0 |
| majority baseline | 0.535 | 0.541 | 0.542 | 0.554 |

Δ(trm_mixer_stepds − every other arm) on the diagonal is +0.15 … +0.47 acc and **8/0 (p=.0078) in
all 16 (T, arm) cells**, including both OOD depths. Per-seed accuracy at T=16:
0.991, 0.995, 0.991, 0.918, 0.886, 0.966, 0.988, 0.995 (every seed far above the 0.554 baseline).

**Reading.**
- **This is the first transferable step operator in the repo.** Trained on T ≤ 8, the step-aligned
  mixer recovers s_12 and s_16 by running 12 and 16 loops: 0.983 / 0.966 per-cell accuracy, 0.886 /
  0.823 whole-row exact-match, where every control is at baseline. §3's "strongest evidence"
  diagnostic passes for this arm.
- **It needs BOTH ingredients — a conjunction, each one-knob-isolated:**
  - the mixing operator: the flat loop with identical supervision fails (0.846 at T=4, baseline beyond);
  - step-aligned supervision: the same mixer with final-state DS under the same curriculum fails
    (0.818 at T=4, baseline beyond); M36 shows single-depth final-target training fails too.
- **Off-diagonal collapse is expected, not a defect.** On a non-convergent CA, R' ≠ T computes
  s_{R'}, the wrong state, so accuracy at baseline off the diagonal is what a correct step operator
  must do. This is a transferable operator, not adaptive compute: the caller must supply T.
- The in-distribution T=8 point (0.992) also dissolves the M3a/M24 "deep CA is unlearnable one-shot
  at T ≥ 8" wall for the mixer — that wall was a supervision limit, not an architecture limit.
- EM decays slowly with depth (0.965 → 0.939 → 0.886 → 0.823) as per-step errors compound; seeds 3
  and 4 are the weaker ones at every depth.

## Caveats (do not overclaim)

1. **Privileged supervision.** Step-aligned DS gives the loop the intermediate states s_1 … s_T —
   strictly more information than a final-target model sees. The claim is "given the trajectory, the
   mixer learns a repeatable local step and iterating it extrapolates", NOT "the loop discovers the
   algorithm from input/output pairs" (M36 says it does not). The flat loop had the same information
   and failed, so the operator — not the information alone — is the active ingredient.
2. **The tying control is not clean under step supervision.** `untied_mixer_matched` ran with DS off
   (as M3b's untied arm did) and cannot be unrolled past depth 8 by construction. Whether an untied
   mixer given per-layer step-aligned readouts matches the tied one at T ≤ 8 is untested. The OOD
   result is still tying-specific in the trivial sense that only a tied loop can run 12 or 16 steps.
   **RESOLVED by M36c below:** with step-aligned DS on both, the tied mixer beats the budget-matched
   untied mixer AND a 7.3×-parameter untied ceiling at T=8 (8/0).
3. **Narrow scope:** one rule (110), one width (24), one curriculum range (1..8), OOD up to 2× T_max.
   Rule 110 is a 3-cell local rule, so a token mixer can represent it exactly; whether this holds for
   wider-neighbourhood or non-uniform rules (`mixed_converge`-style) is open. **PARTLY RESOLVED by
   M36c/M36d below:** OOD now to 4×T_max on rule 110, and rules 30 and 54 replicate (to 2×T_max).
   Width, wider neighbourhoods and non-uniform rules remain open.
4. The single-T run (M36) and the curriculum run (M36b) differ in two knobs (curriculum + alignment);
   the within-M36b `finalds` arm isolates alignment under the curriculum, and M36's arms show a single
   training depth also fails, but a single-depth step-aligned arm was not run.

## M36c — the clean tying control, and extrapolation to 4×T_max (GPU)

Config `configs/experiments/m36c_stepds_tying_control.yaml`; record
`results/m36c_stepds_tying_control_20261024T140111*` (RTX 2070, eager, 5.1 min vs M36b's 13.3 min
on a cloud CPU). Same task, curriculum, epochs and seeds as M36b. New: BOTH untied mixers get
step-aligned DS, and the new opt-in `extrapolation.unroll_untied` evaluates them at min(R', depth)
so a T-step test stops after T blocks (default off; earlier records unchanged; tested in
`tests/test_run.py::test_unroll_untied_evaluates_untied_stack_at_each_r`).

Diagonal R' = T, accuracy / EM (budget: tied 16,058; untied matched 16,208; ceiling 117,712 = 7.3×):

| T | `trm_mixer_stepds` (tied) | `untied_mixer_matched_stepds` | `untied_mixer_stepds` (7.3× ceiling) | `trm_flat_stepds` | `ff_matched` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 1.000 / 0.995 | 0.976 / 0.678 | 1.000 / 0.990 | 0.849 / 0.062 | 0.578 / 0 |
| 8 | **0.998 / 0.973** | 0.689 / 0.002 | 0.950 / 0.396 | 0.600 / 0 | 0.557 / 0 |
| 12 (OOD) | 0.993 / 0.929 | 0.524 / 0 | 0.499 / 0 | 0.515 / 0 | 0.536 / 0 |
| 16 (OOD) | 0.981 / 0.872 | 0.526 / 0 | 0.525 / 0 | 0.516 / 0 | 0.538 / 0 |
| 24 (OOD) | 0.943 / 0.761 | 0.537 / 0 | 0.529 / 0 | 0.517 / 0 | 0.535 / 0 |
| 32 (OOD, 4×T_max) | **0.899 / 0.690** | 0.524 / 0 | 0.511 / 0 | 0.515 / 0 | 0.527 / 0 |

Every tied-vs-other Δ on the diagonal is 8/0 (p=.0078) except tied vs the 7.3× ceiling at T=4
(both ≈1.0, 4/4 tie). Untied arms at T > 8 are capped at their 8 blocks, so they compute s_8 —
baseline by construction; the in-distribution rows are the informative ones.

**Reading.**
- **Tying gives a CAPABILITY here, in distribution, at T=8:** Δ(tied − untied matched) = +0.309 acc /
  +0.971 EM, and the tied loop even beats the 7.3×-parameter untied ceiling (+0.048 acc / +0.577 EM),
  both 8/0. At T=4 the ceiling ties it, and the budget-matched untied stack is already behind
  (+0.024 acc, 8/0). The deeper the block index, the less training signal an untied block gets
  (block i only learns from batches with T ≥ i, and only its own step), while the tied block learns
  from every step of every batch. This is the first place in the program where tying beats an
  untied mixer of equal or larger capacity — but it is specific to step-aligned supervision on a
  uniform rule, where "one shared step" is literally the target.
- **The operator holds to 4×T_max with slow decay:** EM 0.973 (T=8) → 0.872 (16) → 0.761 (24) →
  0.690 (32). Per-seed accuracy at T=32: 0.936, 0.769, 0.947, 0.951, 0.943, 0.731, 0.964, 0.951 — six
  seeds near 0.95, two weaker seeds (1 and 5) that are also the weakest at T=24.
- **Replicates M36b** (T=12 0.993 here vs 0.983 on CPU; T=16 0.981 vs 0.966) — same sign and size,
  small GPU/CPU numeric drift.

## M36e — how much of the trajectory does the operator need? Nearly all of it.

Config `configs/experiments/m36e_stepds_sparsity.yaml`; record `results/m36e_stepds_sparsity_*`
(RTX 2070, 7.7 min). One mixer (16,058 params), the M36c curriculum and seeds; only the supervision
changes. New knob `ds_stride` (default 1 = the old loss; tested bit-identical, and confirmed end to
end: `mixer_stride1` reproduces M36c's `trm_mixer_stepds` exactly, e.g. 0.899 / 0.690 at T=32).

Diagonal R' = T, accuracy / EM:

| arm (supervision) | T=4 | T=8 | T=12 | T=16 | T=24 | T=32 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `mixer_stride1` (every s_i) | 1.000 / 0.995 | 0.998 / 0.973 | 0.993 / 0.929 | 0.981 / 0.872 | 0.943 / 0.761 | 0.899 / 0.690 |
| `mixer_stride2` (s_2, s_4, … + s_T) | 0.969 / 0.702 | 0.855 / 0.278 | 0.693 / 0.022 | 0.575 / 0.002 | 0.509 / 0 | 0.502 / 0 |
| `mixer_stride4` (s_4, s_8 + s_T) | 0.985 / 0.847 | 0.892 / 0.421 | 0.776 / 0.188 | 0.685 / 0.101 | 0.578 / 0.034 | 0.534 / 0.012 |
| `mixer_finalonly` (s_T only, T ~ U{1..8}) | 0.783 / 0.029 | 0.580 / 0 | 0.527 / 0 | 0.520 / 0 | 0.522 / 0 | 0.521 / 0 |
| `mixer_progressive` (Deep Thinking, s_T only) | 0.794 / 0.046 | 0.585 / 0.005 | 0.543 / 0 | 0.544 / 0 | 0.547 / 0 | 0.548 / 0 |
| `ff_matched` | 0.578 / 0 | 0.557 / 0 | 0.536 / 0 | 0.538 / 0 | 0.535 / 0 | 0.527 / 0 |

Δ(stride1 − stride2), Δ(stride1 − stride4), Δ(stride1 − finalonly): 8/0 at every T ≥ 8 (stride2 is
7/1 at T=4). Δ(finalonly − progressive) and both vs `ff_matched` at T ≥ 8: nulls (2/6 … 6/2, |Δ| ≤ 0.03).

**Reading.**
- **The transferable operator needs DENSE trajectory supervision.** Dropping to every 2nd or 4th
  intermediate state keeps a partial in-distribution fit (T=8 acc 0.86–0.89, EM 0.28–0.42) but the
  extrapolation largely goes: EM ≤ 0.10 at T=16 and ≈0 at T=32, where every-state supervision holds
  0.69. Per seed, stride 4 still extrapolates on 2 of 8 seeds (0.925 / 0.909 at T=16) and fails on
  the rest; stride 2 on none. The stride-2 vs stride-4 ordering is noisy (paired at T=16: 6/2 for
  stride 4), so do not read a trend into it — both are far below stride 1.
- **With the final state only, nothing transfers — curriculum or progressive loss.** Supervising only
  s_T (with T varied per batch across 1..8) leaves the mixer near the feedforward control from T=8 on
  (Δ vs ff 5/3, +0.02, ns); in-distribution it only fits shallow depths (T=4 acc 0.78). Deep Thinking
  progressive loss against the final state is indistinguishable from plain final-only (4/4 … 3/5 at
  every T). This repeats the M7 progressive-loss null, now on the mixer.
- **Consequence for the program's question.** The M36 positive is a property of the mixer *given
  every intermediate state*. With input/output pairs only — even across a range of depths — the mixer
  learns neither the step nor a deep circuit on rule 110. Real tabular targets have no trajectory, so
  M36 does not carry over to them as it stands; it shows what the architecture CAN represent and
  iterate, not what it discovers from end-point labels.

## Other things checked in passing

- **The ACT router-initialization trap (arXiv 2604.21999) does not apply to M23.** looptab's halting
  head is trained by BCE to predict correctness, training always runs every segment
  (`train_act`, `src/looptab/train/loop.py:832–846`), and halting is used only at evaluation — so
  halting never sets training depth, which is the trap's precondition.

## What this changes

- §11.2 #4 ("no transferable step operator; no depth-extrapolation") is now FLAT-LOOP / final-target
  specific. On the mixer with step-aligned supervision it is overturned: to 4·T_max on rule 110
  (M36c) and to 2·T_max on rules 30 and 54 (M36d).
- §11.2 #7 ("the win is the ARCHITECTURE, not recurrence; tying adds only efficiency") gets its first
  exception: under step-aligned supervision the tied mixer beats untied mixers of equal and of 7.3×
  capacity in distribution (M36c). Scope: step supervision on a uniform rule.
- M36e answers the supervision question: it is NOT enough. Every-other-state supervision already
  loses most of the extrapolation, and final-state-only training (curriculum or progressive loss)
  learns nothing transferable. The operator is a product of dense step supervision.
- Open next: width (w=48) and wider-neighbourhood / non-uniform rules (cheap, but they extend a
  result that now needs privileged supervision); and the harder question M36e raises — a training
  signal that recovers the step WITHOUT a trajectory, if the program wants a route to targets that
  come without intermediate states. No candidate is validated; note that consistency between R' and
  R'+1 unrolls would be the WRONG target on a non-convergent CA (s_{T+1} ≠ s_T).
